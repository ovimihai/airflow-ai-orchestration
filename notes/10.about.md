### About this module

**Welcome! We're so glad you're here 😍**

  

In [Module 1](https://academy.astronomer.io/ai-orchestation-introduction-for-data-engineers), you learned that Airflow controls the workflow, and AI performs bounded tasks inside it. In this module, you put that rule to work by designing your first AI-aware Dag, step by step.

  

Everything is built around one realistic example: a SaaS company receives customer emails, and some are cancellation requests. Every hour, a pipeline collects new emails, identifies the intent with AI, extracts the requested cancellation date, checks the account, and routes each case to the right team. You'll turn that business process into a Dag with clear tasks, dependencies, and a clean split between deterministic steps and AI steps.

  

You'll then zoom in on the parts that make or break an AI workflow. The XCom contract: what the AI task returns, which fields are required, and what the next task does when the contract is not met. The branching task: the model returns a business label, and your code (not the model) maps that label to the next task. The retry policy: for AI tasks, a retry can cost money and return a different answer, so you'll set retries based on cost, variance, and risk.

  

By the end, you'll have a complete design for an AI-aware Dag: the tasks, the dependencies, the data passed between them, and the rules that keep the workflow in control.

**🎯Objectives**

At the end of this module, you'll be able to:

  

- Turn a business process into an AI-aware Dag sketch with tasks, dependencies, and the data shared between them

- Decide which tasks need AI and which should stay deterministic

- Design an XCom contract for an AI task: required fields, allowed values, what stays out of XCom, and what happens when the contract is not met

- Design a branching task that routes on validated AI output, keeping the label-to-task mapping in your code

- Set a retry policy for an AI task based on cost, variance, and risk, including timeouts and what to do when no usable output comes back
